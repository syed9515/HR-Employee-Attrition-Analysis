## 📌 Project Overview

Employee attrition is an important challenge for organizations because high employee turnover can increase recruitment costs, reduce productivity, and affect workforce stability.

This project analyzes employee attrition using the IBM HR Employee Attrition dataset containing 1,470 employee records.

The analysis was performed using Excel, MySQL, SQL, Power BI, and DAX to identify employee groups and workplace factors associated with higher attrition.

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

The HR department wants to understand:

- How many employees have left the organization?
- What is the overall employee attrition rate?
- Which departments have higher attrition?
- Which job roles and job levels have higher attrition?
- Is overtime associated with higher employee attrition?
- Does business travel relate to employee attrition?
- Which age groups show higher attrition?
- How does job satisfaction relate to employee attrition?

The goal is to turn employee data into actionable insights that can support HR retention strategies.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Clean and prepare the HR dataset using Excel.
- Load the cleaned data into MySQL.
- Perform workforce and attrition analysis using SQL.
- Calculate key HR KPIs using DAX.
- Build an interactive Power BI dashboard.
- Identify employee groups with higher attrition.
- Provide data-driven recommendations for employee retention.

## 🔍 Key Business Insights

### 1. Overtime and Attrition

Employees working overtime have a significantly higher attrition rate than employees who do not work overtime.

- Overtime: **30.53%**
- No Overtime: **10.44%**

This shows a strong association between overtime and employee attrition in this dataset.

### 2. Job Level

Job Level 1 has the highest attrition rate:

- Job Level 1: **26.34%**

This indicates that early-career employees may require additional career development and retention support.

### 3. Department

Sales has the highest department-level attrition:

- Sales: **20.63%**
- Human Resources: **19.05%**
- Research & Development: **13.84%**

### 4. Business Travel

Employees who travel frequently show higher attrition:

- Travel Frequently: **24.91%**
- Travel Rarely: **14.96%**
- Non-Travel: **8.00%**

### 5. Age Group

Younger employee groups show comparatively higher attrition than older groups in several age ranges.

---

## 💡 HR Recommendations

Based on the analysis, the following actions could be considered:

- Review excessive overtime and employee workload.
- Provide stronger career-development opportunities for entry-level employees.
- Investigate the reasons behind higher attrition in the Sales department.
- Review workload and support for employees who travel frequently.
- Strengthen mentoring and career-growth programs for younger employees.
- Improve employee engagement, recognition, and internal mobility.
- Conduct employee feedback or exit interviews to understand the underlying reasons for attrition.

> **Note:** These findings represent associations observed in the dataset and should not be interpreted as proof of causation.
